There are moments in life when we feel exposed, vulnerable to worry, exhaustion, or the unknown ahead. Psalm 121 is a timeless reminder that our true help does not come from what surrounds us, but from the One who made us. This prayer responds to Psalm 121 line by line:

"I lift up my eyes to the hills. From where does my help come? My help comes from the Lord, who made heaven and earth. He will not let your foot be moved; he who keeps you will not slumber. Behold, he who keeps Israel will neither slumber nor sleep. The Lord is your keeper; the Lord is your shade on your right hand. The sun shall not strike you by day, nor the moon by night. The Lord will keep you from all evil; he will keep your life. The Lord will keep your going out and your coming in from this time forth and forevermore."

Lord, I lift my eyes to you because I know where my help comes from. It comes from you—who made heaven and earth, who breathed stars into the sky and shaped mountains with a whisper. My help comes from you surely and constantly. You will not let my foot be moved: even when I walk with trembling steps, you steady me and uphold me. Because you do not sleep or slumber, you never take breaks, turn away, or blink. You who kept Abraham, Joseph, Ruth, and David keep me too.

You are my Keeper—the One who stays when others walk away. You are my shade at my right hand: close, present, real, covering me when pressures feel like scorching heat. The sun will not strike me by day, nor the moon by night: you know what drains me in the brightness and what haunts me in the dark, and you shelter me in every shift from light to dark.

You keep me from all evil—shielding my heart from arrows of accusation, bitterness, and hopelessness. You keep my actual life, my soul, and my identity. And you keep my going out and my coming in: every step, every errand, every meeting, every hallway, every breath, from this time forth and forevermore. So I lift my eyes beyond the hills to you, Lord—my Maker, my Keeper, my Shade, my Watchman, my Strength, my Help, my God. Amen.
